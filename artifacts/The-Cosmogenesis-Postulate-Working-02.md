# The Cosmogenesis Postulate

## A Candidate Unified Origin for Quantum Mechanics, General Relativity, Electromagnetism, and Accelerated Expansion

Author: Andres Quesada  
Date: 07-20-26  
Status: Working physical postulate and research framework

## Executive Summary

The Cosmogenesis Postulate proposes that the quantum mechanical force, the gravitational force, and the forces exerted by dark energy and dark matter are fundamental to a shared multiverse substrate, while general relativity is an emergent framework that is not necessarily constant across the multiverse. The substrate is not located inside space and does not evolve within a presumed universal clock. Space, time, causal order, matter, fields, and universe-specific physical laws emerge when the substrate undergoes a nucleating phase transition.

The physical picture begins with a phone book the size of a table. A torch burns through the top-left edge until ash begins to emerge from the back. A second torch burns through the bottom-right edge, producing more ash. The ash from the two burns may intersect and accumulate. When enough material comes together, it may form a universe.

The book represents the shared multiverse substrate. Imagine quasars as the torches of this fundamental substrate. Their immense energy penetrates its organized structure until information passes beyond the observable reach of the originating universe and enters the state represented by ash. Ash from separate quasars may intersect and accumulate. When enough material comes together, it may form a new universe. The hidden back is not another cosmic room. It represents the same substrate beyond the reach of current physics.

That formation event is nucleation. When enough ash accumulates, the substrate crosses a threshold and forms a new universe. Nucleation does not scatter matter into preexisting space. It establishes the local framework in which space, time, matter, fields, and physical laws become defined. In our universe, that framework includes spacetime and general relativity, through which the fundamental forces of the substrate are expressed.

# 1. Purpose and Book Analogy

## 1.1. Purpose

The purpose of this document is to state a conceptually simple candidate for grand unification and identify the work required to test it. The postulate treats the quantum mechanical force, gravitational force, and forces exerted by dark energy and dark matter as fundamental to one shared multiverse substrate, while spacetime, general relativity, and universe-specific laws emerge through nucleation.

## 1.2. The Mapping

| Physical image | Theoretical meaning |
| --- | --- |
| The phone book | The shared multiverse substrate |
| The paper | Fundamental substrate material |
| The same paper throughout | Common fundamental forces |
| Bound pages | An organized universe |
| The arrangement of pages | Universe-specific physical laws |
| The front of the book | The observable universe |
| The depth of the book | Inaccessible substrate structure |
| The hidden back | Reality beyond current physics |
| A quasar as a torch | A black-hole energy source |
| Heat entering the paper | Concentrated energy and information |
| The burn moving inward | Substrate transformation |
| The burn reaching the far side | Information leaving observable physics |
| The hole left behind | The black-hole interface |
| Ash | Information in a substrate state |
| Ash from separate burns | Output from multiple black holes |
| Ash intersecting | Substrate interaction |
| Ash accumulating | Pre-universe concentration |
| Critical accumulation | The nucleation threshold |
| Sudden formation | The Big Bang |
| The new structure | A sibling universe |
| The structure’s geometry | Emergent spacetime |
| The structure’s internal rules | Universe-specific physical laws |
| Force acting through the common material | Fundamental gravity |
| How the structure bends under that force | General relativity in our universe |

The analogy's first important statement is that organization changes while the underlying substrate remains. An intact page can support printed language, ordering, and reference. Ash cannot support those same structures, even though it is made from the same material. Under the postulate, a universe's spacetime and laws similarly depend on a particular organization of the substrate. Burn-through removes information from that organization without requiring its destruction.

The analogy's second important statement is that current physics captures only the part of the process expressed within our universe. We can observe matter and energy entering a black hole, but we cannot yet describe or measure how that process continues through the shared substrate. This continuation is not located elsewhere and is not separated from our universe by an external boundary. It remains part of the same fundamental substrate and may become observable through a more complete physical theory.

The analogy’s third important statement is that ash is not already a universe. A universe begins when enough ash accumulates to cross a threshold and nucleate. Nucleation does not occur within preexisting space. It establishes the spacetime and local physical laws of the new universe.

Universes form as distinct organizations of the same shared substrate. Ash from many black holes may contribute to the same nucleation event, and each new universe emerges from that common accumulation.

## 1.3. Limits of the Analogy

The postulate does not treat the substrate as inaccessible in principle. Its present inaccessibility reflects the limits of current theory and instrumentation, not a permanent barrier to observation or interaction.

In the analogy, a quasar represents the immense concentration of energy surrounding an active black hole. That energy drives the transformation of the substrate represented by the book burning into ash. The analogy does not claim that matter literally burns or that a quasar alone produces a universe.

# 2. Ontology

## 2.1. The Substrate

The substrate is the common foundation from which every universe forms. It does not begin with space, dimensions, or a universal clock. Its exact mathematical form remains unknown and may eventually be represented by a graph, hypergraph, operator algebra, or another relational structure. Every universe and every unobservable substrate state is a different organization of the same substrate.

## 2.2. Uniformity

The substrate is uniform, so every universe forms from the same fundamental basis even if its emergent physics differs.

## 2.3. Organized Domains

An organized domain is a stable organization of the substrate that sustains its own internal structure and locally fundamental laws. A universe remains distinct for as long as that organization persists. In our universe, this includes stable causal order and consistent physical observations across scales.

## 2.4. Unrendered States

An unrendered state is a configuration of the shared substrate that is not governed or described by the physics of a particular universe. Ash is the working name for the information-bearing substrate state produced when a black-hole system burns through the substrate and propagates information into a domain where current physics cannot operate. The ash remains part of the same substrate, but it is no longer represented by the physical framework of the originating universe.

## 2.5. Universes and Relational Repulsion

A universe is a stable organization of the shared substrate. Nucleation gives it its own spacetime, matter, fields, and locally fundamental physical laws. Those laws determine how that universe expresses and responds to the forces fundamental to the substrate. Sibling universes may differ internally while remaining connected through the same substrate. In this postulate, each sibling universe exerts a repulsive effect on ours through that shared substrate.

# 3. Recursive Cosmogenesis

The substrate gives rise to universes, and those universes continue to affect the same substrate. Their black holes return information to the substrate as ash, which may contribute to the nucleation of later universes. Cosmogenesis is recursive because each generation of universes participates in the formation of the next.

# 4. Black-Hole Burn-Through and Information Preservation

## 4.1. Burn-Through

Black holes are candidate interfaces between the organization of our universe and the shared substrate. The event horizon is a causal boundary within our spacetime. Burn-through is the additional hypothesized process by which a black-hole system transforms information out of our universe's physical representation and into the substrate state called ash.

Current physics describes the observable side of this process, including infall, accretion, radiation, curvature, and the event horizon. The postulate proposes that the process continues through the shared substrate beyond what current physics can presently describe or measure. A complete theory must reproduce established black-hole behavior in every regime where general relativity and quantum field theory already succeed.

## 4.2. Information Preservation

Burn-through transforms information rather than destroying it. A mathematical theory must identify what remains represented within our universe, what becomes ash, and what invariant accounts for the complete information before and after the transition.

The theory must also reconcile infalling information, black-hole entropy, Hawking radiation, the ash state, and any remaining black-hole degrees of freedom. Until that accounting is defined, information preservation is a requirement of the postulate rather than a demonstrated result.

# 5. Sibling Repulsion and Accelerated Expansion

## 5.1. Cumulative Repulsion

Every sibling universe exerts a repulsive effect on ours through the shared substrate. Because cosmogenesis is recursive, the population of sibling universes may continue to grow. The postulate proposes that their accumulated repulsion is the source of the accelerated expansion observed in our universe.

The magnitude of an individual contribution and the rule by which contributions combine remain unknown. The mechanism may involve locality, screening, saturation, decay, or another substrate behavior that has not yet been derived.

## 5.2. Large-Scale Smoothness

Individual repulsive contributions may be irregular, while the expansion observed across large scales is approximately smooth and isotropic. A complete theory must explain how many substrate-level contributions combine into that large-scale behavior.

The theory must also determine whether small residual anisotropies, correlations, or fluctuations remain. Invoking an enormous or infinite population is not enough. The averaging process and its measurable consequences must be defined.

## 5.3. Observation Within Our Universe

Sibling repulsion acts through the shared substrate at the level of whole universe domains. It is not ordinary matter, a conventional electromagnetic field, or a substance distributed through our spacetime.

Our universe responds through the geometry established during its nucleation. From inside our universe, that response is observed as accelerated expansion. General relativity describes the geometric response within our universe. It is not the fundamental cross-domain force producing the repulsion.

A future mathematical treatment may represent the observed response as an effective stress-energy contribution when useful. That representation would be an internal description of the effect, not evidence for a separate material substance.

## 5.4. Required Quantification

A complete model must derive the connection between sibling universes and the expansion history measured within ours. It must determine:

- what sets the magnitude of each repulsive relationship;
- how the contributions add, screen, saturate, or decay;
- how an unbounded population produces a finite observable effect;
- how substrate-level repulsion changes the geometry of our universe;
- why the resulting expansion is smooth and isotropic at observed scales;
- what residual fluctuations or correlations should remain; and
- whether the calculated expansion agrees with observation without inserting the desired result by hand.

# 6. Requirements for Mathematical Development

## 6.1. The Substrate and Quantum Mechanics

The exact mathematical form of the substrate remains open. Graphs, hypergraphs, operator algebras, quantum networks, and other relational structures are possible tools, not commitments of the postulate.

Any proposed substrate model must reproduce the defining behavior of quantum mechanics rather than merely attach quantum language to a classical structure. It must account for interference, entanglement, outcome probabilities, composition of systems, no-signaling behavior, and the emergence of stable classical records in the regimes where those phenomena are observed.

## 6.2. Burn-Through and Nucleation

A mathematical development must define the ash state, the way ash contributions interact, the condition under which accumulation becomes nucleation-ready, and the transition by which a new universe forms.

Those definitions cannot assume preexisting space, ordinary time, or the internal physics of the universe they are meant to produce. The nucleation process must establish spacetime and universe-specific physical laws rather than selecting a desired result after the fact.

## 6.3. Fundamental Gravity and Emergent General Relativity

The mathematical model must represent the quantum mechanical force, gravitational force, and forces exerted by dark energy and dark matter as properties of the shared substrate. It must then explain how nucleation gives our universe its spacetime, matter, fields, electromagnetism, and locally fundamental laws.

General relativity must emerge as the way our universe responds to and expresses fundamental gravity. It must not be treated as gravity itself. A sibling universe may express the same fundamental substrate forces through a different internal framework.

## 6.4. A Finite Effect From an Unbounded Population

The postulate allows an unbounded or infinite process of universe formation. A complete theory must therefore supply a measure or interaction rule that produces a finite repulsive effect within our universe.

The answer may involve locality, changing strength with substrate relationship, screening, saturation, equilibrium, or another mechanism. The effect cannot simply be declared finite because the observed expansion is finite.

## 6.5. Recovery of Established Physics

A successful model must recover established physics in every regime where existing theories have been tested. It must reproduce quantum-mechanical behavior, general relativity, electromagnetism, black-hole thermodynamics, and the observed large-scale expansion of our universe.

Producing a geometry that resembles spacetime is not enough. Producing labels that resemble fields is not enough. The model must reproduce the measured behavior and relationships those theories describe before the postulate can become a physical theory.

# 7. Experimental Program

## 7.1. Minimal Substrate Model

Begin with the smallest substrate model capable of supporting nonclassical behavior. Before assigning cosmological meaning to it, test whether it can reproduce interference, entanglement, outcome probabilities, composition of systems, isolated evolution, and no-signaling behavior.

This separates a genuinely quantum substrate from a classical information system wearing quantum terminology.

## 7.2. Burn-Through Accounting

Construct a simplified burn-through process with an explicit information invariant. Verify that information removed from a modeled universe remains represented in the ash state and that the complete process is compatible with the selected quantum framework.

The internal side of the model must be compared with established black-hole constraints before the process is interpreted as a physical black-hole interior or a solution to the information problem.

## 7.3. Ash Accumulation and Nucleation

Define how ash contributions interact and what condition marks the nucleation threshold without referring to the universe one hopes to obtain. Determine whether the model produces a genuine phase transition and whether stable universe domains form reproducibly.

The tests should measure:

- the stability of accumulated ash;
- the behavior near the nucleation threshold;
- sensitivity to the mixture of contributing black holes;
- whether different mixtures produce similar classes of universes; and
- whether a universe forms without inserting its target structure by hand.

## 7.4. Repulsion and Expansion

Generate multiple stable universe domains and define their repulsive relationships to the modeled version of our universe. Freeze the interaction rule before measuring the geometric response.

The experiment should test whether additional siblings increase the total repulsive effect, whether the effect saturates or becomes screened, whether large numbers of contributions become smooth and isotropic, and whether residual fluctuations remain. Controls should include no repulsion, attraction, randomized strengths, randomized membership, and disabled interactions.

The final readout must connect the substrate interaction to a measurable change in the internal geometry of the modeled universe and compare that change with cosmological observations.

## 7.5. Confirmatory Discipline

Exploratory work may search for useful state variables, thresholds, interaction laws, or observables. Once discovered, those relationships become hypotheses and must be tested in new runs.

Confirmatory tests must fix the rules, thresholds, primary observables, controls, exclusions, and analysis before the result is measured. Held-out systems must be used, and failed or negative results must be retained.

# 8. Predictions and Failure Conditions

## 8.1. Predictions

If the postulate is viable, mathematical and experimental development should reveal:

- an information-bearing ash state produced through black-hole burn-through;
- a substrate-defined threshold at which accumulated ash nucleates;
- stable universes formed without a one-black-hole-to-one-universe relationship;
- locally fundamental laws established through nucleation;
- repulsive relationships between our universe and its siblings;
- a growing cumulative repulsive effect as contributing siblings increase within an unsaturated regime;
- accelerated expansion without a visible substance distributed through our spacetime;
- a finite rule governing the effect of an unbounded sibling population; and
- possible residual anisotropies, correlations, or fluctuations associated with discrete sibling contributions.

The postulate does not yet provide numerical amplitudes, a finished aggregation law, or a quantitative expansion curve. Those are required developments, not present predictions disguised as calculations.

## 8.2. Failure Conditions

The postulate should be rejected, narrowed, or substantially revised if:

- no information-preserving burn-through process can be defined consistently with quantum mechanics and black-hole thermodynamics;
- no meaningful ash state can be distinguished from ordinary information loss;
- ash cannot accumulate or cross a substrate-defined nucleation threshold;
- stable universes form only when their desired structure is inserted by hand;
- the model cannot produce the spacetime, quantum behavior, matter, fields, and physical laws observed in our universe;
- general relativity can be recovered only by identifying it with fundamental gravity rather than deriving it as our universe's response;
- sibling repulsion always diverges, vanishes, or becomes physically meaningless;
- the repulsive interaction necessarily permits observable nonlocal signaling within our universe;
- accelerated expansion appears only after tuning the interaction law to produce it;
- the model fails to reproduce general relativity, electromagnetism, quantum mechanics, or black-hole behavior in tested regimes; or
- the predicted expansion history, isotropy, structure growth, or residual correlations conflict with observation.

# 9. Relationship to Existing Work

Existing theories may provide useful mathematical instruments without proving the Cosmogenesis Postulate. The postulate remains distinct because it combines black-hole burn-through, information-bearing ash, nucleation of sibling universes, fundamental substrate forces, emergent general relativity, and cumulative sibling repulsion.

| Component | Neighboring work | What may be borrowed | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emergent locality and gauge behavior | Quantum graphity[[1]](#ref-1)[[2]](#ref-2) | Examples of geometry, locality, and gauge behavior emerging from a nongeometric structure | Black-hole burn-through, ash, universe nucleation, or sibling repulsion |
| Condensate cosmology | Group-field-theory condensates[[3]](#ref-3) | Examples of cosmological behavior emerging from quantum condensate states | Ash produced by black holes or the formation and repulsion of sibling universes |
| Discrete causal geometry | Causal set theory[[4]](#ref-4) | A possible mathematical language for causal order if later development supports it | The substrate's required form or a derivation of our universe from ash |
| Curvature diagnostics | Causal-set curvature and manifold tests[[5]](#ref-5)[[6]](#ref-6)[[7]](#ref-7) | Possible instruments for testing a discrete spacetime model | Evidence that the substrate must be a causal set or that repulsion produces our geometry |
| Black holes and cosmic expansion | Cosmologically coupled black-hole research[[8]](#ref-8)[[9]](#ref-9) | Evidence that relationships between black holes and expansion can be investigated observationally | Burn-through, ash, sibling universes, or cumulative substrate repulsion |

# 10. Open Research Questions

- What is the simplest mathematical object capable of representing the shared substrate?
- How does that object reproduce interference, entanglement, outcome probabilities, and stable classical records?
- How can change occur in the substrate without assuming a universal clock?
- What physical condition marks complete black-hole burn-through?
- What information-bearing state is represented by ash?
- What invariant accounts for information across infall, Hawking radiation, the remaining black hole, and ash?
- How do ash contributions interact without assuming ordinary space and time?
- What determines the nucleation threshold?
- How does nucleation establish spacetime and locally fundamental laws?
- How does fundamental gravity act through the substrate before a universe-specific spacetime exists?
- How does general relativity emerge as our universe's response to that gravity?
- How do electromagnetism, matter, and fields emerge within our universe?
- What determines the strength of a sibling universe's repulsive effect on ours?
- How do an unbounded number of siblings produce a finite total effect?
- Why does cumulative repulsion appear smooth and isotropic at large scales?
- What residual fluctuations, anisotropies, or correlations should remain?
- What mathematical relationship connects sibling repulsion to the expansion rate measured within our universe?
- Can the model reproduce the observed expansion history without fitting the mechanism to the desired result?
- What observation could distinguish sibling repulsion from a cosmological constant, modified gravity, evolving dark energy, or cosmologically coupled black holes?
- Can a universe eventually lose its stable organization and return all of its information to the substrate?
